## SECTION 2 — WHAT THE GOVERNMENT IS BUYING (PWS Snapshot)

CDC OCIO is acquiring an integrated, comprehensive set of IT customer support and IT management services for ~28,000 CDC personnel across CONUS, OCONUS, and field locations. The current model is a tiered ITSM service delivery structure (Level 1 / Level 2 / Level 3) using ITIL as the standard and **ServiceNow as the platform of record** for ticketing, knowledge management, and customer support.

### Stated Objectives (from PWS Section 2)

1. Satisfy customer IT needs with timely, effective, and excellent customer support.
2. Leverage emerging technology and lessons learned to continuously improve the end-to-end service experience.
3. Capitalize on IT service management best practices (ITIL, PMBOK).
4. **Reduce OCIO total cost of ownership consistently over time.**
5. Comply with all CDC security requirements.
6. Provide customer support across all CDC CONUS and OCONUS locations.
7. Evaluate, recommend, and implement new advances in technology.

## SECTION 3 — THE EVALUATION (Section F)

This is a Best Value tradeoff (FAR 16.505) where **Technical + Past Performance combined are more important than Price.** When technical proposals are considered technically equal, price becomes the deciding factor.

### Technical Factor Sub-Factors (in the order listed in the RFP)

1. **Technical Approach** — Understanding, methodology, approach for each task; project plan; problem statement and risks; **three (3) test-and-learn pilot ideas with expected benefits and challenges.**
2. **Staffing Plan** — Labor categories, hours, qualifications, certifications, clearances, retention, turnover rates for past two years, Small Business Subcontracting Plan, subcontractor descriptions.
3. **Similar Experience** — **Three (3) relevant contracts** similar in scope, size, complexity, performed within the **last three (3) years.** (Note: this is part of the Technical volume's Similar Experience requirement and is distinct from the separate Past Performance volume.)
4. **Management Approach** — Org structure, decision-making authority, performance measures, risk management, telework oversight, conflict management, client satisfaction plan, fiscal responsibility, **Quality Control Plan (draft), Transition-In Plan (draft).**
5. **Transition Plan** — Phase-in and phase-out, technical and management approach, timeline, resources, risks (cost, technical, performance), mitigation strategies, no service interruption or degradation.

- Government will assess relative performance risk based on: conformance to specifications, adherence to schedules, customer satisfaction, business-like concern for customer interests.
- Government may use information beyond what offerors submit (CPARS, other federal contract POCs).

## SECTION 5 — WIN THEMES (USE THESE TO THREAD THROUGH EVERY VOLUME)

These are the approved win themes for this pursuit. They are framed for **client benefit first**, not Deloitte capability first. When you draft, anchor your language to these themes and to specific PWS requirements. Do not invent new themes. Do not water these down with generic language.

### Win Theme 1 — A Better, More Cost-Efficient Service for ~28,000 CDC Users

**The CDC's ask:** Reduce total cost of ownership consistently over time while improving the customer experience.

**Our answer:** A modernized service model that drives measurable cost reduction through automation, ServiceNow optimization, AI-assisted resolution, and self-service deflection — without sacrificing service quality or breaking what works today.

**Where to thread:** Technical Approach, Test-and-Learn Pilots, Continuous Improvement (Task 12), Management Approach (fiscal responsibility), Transition Plan (no degradation during ramp).

**Avoid:** "Cost savings" as a slogan. Anchor every claim to a specific lever (deflection rate, automation of password resets, ServiceNow workflow efficiency, etc.) and a specific operational baseline from PWS Tables 3 and 4.

### Win Theme 2 — Human-Centered Design as the Operating Model, Not a Slogan

**The CDC's ask:** The PWS itself names "human-centered design" as part of CDC's IT modernization strategy (Section 1.1) and uses Customer Experience (CX) and Customer Experience Office (CEO) terminology throughout. CDC has already signaled this matters to them.

**Our answer:** HCD is woven through every aspect of how we deliver — service desk experience design, journey-mapped support flows for distinct CDC populations (scientists, field responders, program staff, global partners), accessibility as design input from day one (Section 508 / WCAG 2.0 AA), and continuous voice-of-the-customer measurement that drives real changes in service delivery.

**Where to thread:** Technical Approach (every task description), Management Approach (continuous improvement and CX measurement), Test-and-Learn Pilots, Transition Plan (CDC users are users of the transition itself), Similar Experience narratives.

**Avoid:** Treating HCD as a section. It must show up across every task. Avoid "human-centered design" as a buzzword — name specific methods (journey mapping, contextual inquiry, service blueprinting, usability testing, co-design) and tie each to a specific operational outcome.

### Win Theme 3 — Operational Excellence: Pain-Point Reduction Anchored to CDC's Real Numbers

**The CDC's ask:** Resolve technology issues quickly. Maintain SLAs (e.g., <2 minute average speed-to-answer, <3% error rate, 96%+ customer satisfaction, 80%+ agent availability, <3% missed calls). Reduce inefficiencies. Modernize the ITSD.

**Our answer:** Specific, named pain points addressed with specific automation and process improvements. Self-service for the 7,000 annual LAN password resets. Automated provisioning workflow for the 4,300 annual new user accounts. ServiceNow knowledge management that actually deflects tickets instead of creating them.

**Where to thread:** Technical Approach (Tasks 1.1, 1.2, 8.1, 8.2, 12.1), Staffing Plan (right-sized to operational reality), Management Approach (QCP performance measures beyond PWS), Test-and-Learn Pilots.

**Avoid:** Generic operational excellence language. Every claim must be anchored to a specific PWS task, a specific SLA, or a specific number from PWS Tables 3 and 4.

### Win Theme 4 — Modernization & Innovation, Practically Applied

**The CDC's ask:** Leverage AI, RPA, ServiceNow automation, and emerging technologies to improve operations and CX. Three test-and-learn pilots required.

**Our answer:** Three concrete, well-scoped test-and-learn pilot ideas (TBD by capture team but should hit the highest-volume pain points: e.g., AI-assisted password reset, automated provisioning, intelligent ticket triage). Each pilot has clear expected benefits, named challenges, measurement approach, and a path to scale.

**Where to thread:** Technical Approach, Test-and-Learn section, Management Approach (innovation governance).

**Avoid:** Promising "AI" without specifying use case, training data, integration with ServiceNow, or measurement approach. Avoid claims that imply rip-and-replace of CDC's ITSM platform — ServiceNow is the platform of record.

### Win Theme 5 — Risk-Managed Transition with Embedded Continuity (Our GDIT Advantage)

**The CDC's ask:** A 30-day transition (2/1/2025 – 2/28/2025), FFP, with no interruption or degradation of service levels. Working with the incumbent. Minimize impact to ~28,000 CDC users.

**Our answer:** Our team includes GDIT, currently a subcontractor on the existing CITS contract, providing institutional continuity — the people, the platforms, and the operational memory — while introducing fresh prime leadership focused on modernization, HCD, and cost efficiency. **This is a unique differentiator the prime incumbent cannot match: continuity without complacency.**

**Where to thread:** Transition Plan (centerpiece), Management Approach (transition-in plan), Staffing Plan (retained operational knowledge), Similar Experience (where Deloitte and GDIT have worked together previously, if applicable).

**Avoid:** Trashing the prime incumbent. Mary (Jane Doe persona) and the panel are neutral on them. Frame this as "continuity through GDIT's deep CITS knowledge, energized by Deloitte's prime leadership and modernization expertise." The GDIT angle de-risks the transition story without requiring offensive positioning.

### Win Theme 6 — Federal Health "Gold Standard" Help Desk Experience, Brought to CDC

**The CDC's ask:** Demonstrate similar experience and the ability to deliver outstanding IT customer service.

**Our answer:** Deloitte brings broad enterprise CDC experience across multiple programs combined with help desk and ITSM operations recognized as gold-standard at adjacent federal health agencies (NIH, CMS, FDA, VA — capture team to confirm specific references). ## SECTION 7 — VOICE, TONE, AND WRITING DISCIPLINE

This is the section that matters most for AI-assisted drafting. AI tools default to consulting-speak unless you explicitly tell them not to. Always include this voice guidance in your prompt.

### THE GOVERNING PRINCIPLE: CLIENT FIRST, NOT US FIRST

Every paragraph should be readable as: **"Here is what CDC gets / how this helps CDC / how this addresses CDC's specific situation."** Not: "Here is what Deloitte does / Deloitte's methodology / Deloitte's capabilities."

Speak to our strengths through the lens of CDC outcomes, never as standalone capability claims.

### TONE TARGETS

- **Confident but humble.** We have what it takes to do this work. We do not need to brag about it.
- **Specific over general.** Every claim anchored to a PWS requirement, a number from CDC's own data, a named tool, or a concrete method.
- **Direct.** Short sentences. Active voice. Front-load the point.
- **Mission-aligned.** CDC's mission is public health. Every IT capability we describe ultimately serves ~28,000 CDC personnel who serve the American public. Reflect that.
- **Plain English.** A CDC scientist or operational manager should understand every paragraph without a dictionary.

### WORDS AND PHRASES TO ELIMINATE (BUZZWORD KILL LIST)

Do not use any of the following — they signal consulting fluff and erode credibility with both Jane and John:

- "Best-in-class"
- "World-class"
- "Industry-leading"
- "Partner of choice"
- "Trusted partner"
- "Seamless"
- "Seamlessly integrate"
- "White-glove"
- "Next-generation"
- "Transformative" / "transformational"
- "Cutting-edge"
- "Bleeding-edge"
- "Synergy" / "synergies"
- "Leverage" (as a verb meaning "use") — use "use" or "apply"
- "Holistic"
- "Robust" (overused; usually meaningless)
- "Mission-critical" (acceptable rarely; usually filler)
- "End-to-end" (acceptable rarely; only when it adds meaning)
- "Drive" (as in "drive efficiencies") — name what you are actually doing
- "Unleash"
- "Empower"
- "Reimagine"
- "Re-envision"
- "Journey" (when not literally a customer journey map)
- "Unlock value"
- "Deliver value"
- "Value-add"
- "Solutioning" (not a word)
- "Operationalize" (use "put into operation" or "deploy")
- "Game-changing"
- "Paradigm shift"
- "Move the needle"
- "Lean in"
- "Wheelhouse"
- "Sweet spot"
- "Low-hanging fruit"

### CONSULTING-ISMS TO AVOID

- Starting sentences with "Our approach is to..." — instead, lead with what CDC gets.
- "We will leverage our extensive experience to..." — replace with the specific experience and the specific application.
- "Our proven methodology..." — name the methodology and what it does.
- "Centers of excellence" — describe the actual capability instead.
- "Thought leadership" — irrelevant to a federal proposal.
- "Powered by Deloitte" — never. This is CDC's program, not ours.
- Any sentence that uses "Deloitte" more than once per paragraph. (Run a find for "Deloitte" in your draft and ask: can this paragraph make the same point with one mention or none?)

### NAMING DISCIPLINE

- **First reference:** "Deloitte and our subcontractor team, including GDIT" or "Our team, led by Deloitte with GDIT as a key subcontractor."
- **Subsequent references:** "the Team," "our team," or specific role names (e.g., "the Service Desk Lead").
- Avoid "Deloitte" as the subject of every sentence. Use task names, role names, or "the Team" instead.

### POSITIVE LANGUAGE TARGETS (USE FREELY)

- Specific PWS task numbers when relevant ("Per PWS Task 1.1...").
- Specific operational baselines ("CDC's 7,000 annual LAN password resets...").
- Specific named platforms ("Using ServiceNow's [feature]...").
- Specific SLAs ("Maintaining the <2 minute average speed-to-answer required under PWS Section 4...").
- Specific user populations ("CDC scientists, field responders during outbreak response, OCONUS staff, and program offices each have distinct support patterns. We address each by...").
- "We will" (commitment) over "We can" (capability statement).
- "CDC will benefit from..." or "This means for CDC..." framing.

## SECTION 8 — PROTEST-DEFENSIBILITY GUARDRAILS

Every claim in the proposal will be evaluated; some may be challenged in a debrief or protest. Hold to these rules:

1. **Do not overclaim.** If we have not done it, do not say we have. If we have done something adjacent, say so precisely. ("We have delivered help desk operations for [X federal health agency]. We are bringing that experience, combined with GDIT's institutional knowledge of CITS, to CDC.")
2. **Do not invent past performance.** Past performance references must be real, recent (within 3 years), and verifiable. Capture team confirms references; writers do not.
3. **Quantified claims must be sourced.** "Reduced ticket volume by 30%" requires either a real past performance reference or a clearly framed projection ("Based on [methodology], we project X% reduction over [timeframe], measured by [metric]").
4. **Do not attribute capabilities to specific named subs without their written approval.** Capture team manages all sub-prime statements.
5. **Avoid claims about competitors.** Do not name them, do not characterize them, do not imply weakness. Mary and John are neutral; trashing the incumbent loses both.
6. **Section L compliance is non-negotiable.** Page count, font, margins, file format. A non-compliant proposal can be discarded.
7. **Match assumptions to PWS exactly.** If we make an assumption, label it clearly and ensure it does not "adversely impact or affect the Government's requirement" (Section E specifically reserves the right to reject such proposals).
8. **Cross-volume consistency.** Numbers, names, dates, and approaches must match across Technical, Past Performance, and Business volumes. Inconsistencies are protest fodder.
9. **Do not cite training data or external sources speculatively.** If you reference an industry stat or framework, capture team confirms the citation before submission.
10. **Avoid statements that constrain future flexibility.** Phrases like "we will use [Tool X] exclusively" can backfire if CDC asks for alternatives later. Use "Our default approach uses [Tool X]; we will adapt as CDC requires."

- **Level 1:** IT Service Desk (ITSD) — first contact, ServiceNow-based, phone/email/chat/portal channels.
- **Level 2:** Deskside Support Services (DSS) — Customer Support Center (CSC) and Field Site Support (FSS) teams.
- **Level 3:** Specialists.

### Platforms and Tools (named in PWS)
- **ServiceNow** — ITSM platform of record (tracking, knowledge, self-service).
- **Microsoft 365** including Outlook, Teams, mailboxes (M365 / GCC environment).
- **Zscaler / CITGO (CDC IT on the GO)** — Remote access.
- **Citrix** — Used in remote access context.
- **CyberArk** — Privileged access / MFA-related.
- **MECM** — Microsoft Endpoint Configuration Manager (deployment/imaging).
- **Smartcards / PIV** — Identity credentials.

### Locations and Population
- **~28,000 CDC personnel globally.**
- **OCIO:** ~375 federal employees + ~1,275 contractors.
- **Atlanta locations:** Century Center, Chamblee, Corporate Square, District at Chamblee, Lawrenceville (Non-Response Area), Roybal.

- **Non-CDC supported locations:** BIDS program, Bio-Watch, EPA regional offices (11 regions), Health department labs and clinics, Indian Health Services offices, Vessel Sanitation Program, others.
- **OCONUS:** Global Activities supports CDC presence outside the continental US.

### CDC Mission Surge Reality
PWS explicitly references public health emergencies (Zika, Ebola named as examples) where staffing levels at PHS locations may increase, requiring daily on-site support until the event ends. Field Site Support teams must be ready to travel and remain on-site. **Surge capacity is not theoretical — it is mission-critical.**

The Contractor must execute test-and-learn pilots during option periods. **Each option period:** submit 3 test-and-learn project ideas to pilot an innovation. Test-and-learns must improve customer experience and improve operational efficiency. OCIO selects pilots and has final decision authority on whether to scale.

> **For the proposal:** Section E instructions require **three (3) test-and-learn pilot ideas as part of the technical approach response, including expected benefits and challenges.** This is a hard requirement — do not omit.

## SECTION 14 — DRAFTING WORKFLOW: HOW TO PROMPT THE AI EFFECTIVELY

This is the practical step-by-step for writers who are new to the pursuit. Follow this every time.

### Step 1 — Set up the AI session
1. Open a fresh AI session (not a continuation of yesterday's chat — fresh).
2. Paste this entire Master Context Pack at the top.
3. Confirm the AI received it: *"Confirm you have received the CITS Master Context Pack and summarize back: (a) the win themes, (b) the page limit, (c) the buzzword kill list."* If it can't summarize correctly, the pack didn't fully load — paste again or split.

### Step 2 — Add your specific section context
Paste:
- The exact PWS sub-section(s) you are drafting for.
- The exact Section E instruction(s) that govern your section.
- Any prior-draft text you are revising (or a blank slate notation).

### Step 3 — Prompt for the draft
Use a prompt like:

> *"Draft Section [X] of the CITS Technical Volume covering PWS Task [Y]. Apply the win themes from the Master Context Pack — especially [Win Theme #s relevant to this section]. Use specific PWS numbers (population, ticket volumes, SLAs) from Section 13 of the pack. Avoid every word on the buzzword kill list. Write client-first (CDC outcomes, not Deloitte capabilities). Keep tone direct, specific, and confident-but-humble. Target [X] words. Do not invent past performance, named personnel, or numbers."*

### Step 4 — Stress-test the draft against personas
After the AI produces a draft, run:

> *"Now read this draft as Jane Doe (former CXO, CDC senior leader, neutral on incumbent, allergic to fluff, HCD-as-identity). What lands, what annoys her, and what would she ask for more of? Be specific."*

Then:

> *"Now read it as John Doe (long-tenured CITS day-to-day ops leader, stability-first, allergic to marketing language, lives in operational metrics). What lands, what annoys him, and what would he ask for more of?"*

### Step 5 — Compliance pass
Run:

> *"Check this draft against the Section E instructions for Section [X]. List every required element from Section E, and tell me which are addressed, which are partially addressed, and which are missing."*

### Step 6 — Buzzword pass
Run:

> *"Audit this draft against the buzzword kill list in Section 7 of the Master Context Pack. List every instance of a banned word or phrase, and propose a specific replacement that says the same thing in plain language."*

### Step 7 — "Deloitte first" pass
Run:

> *"Find every sentence in this draft that leads with Deloitte, our team, or our capabilities rather than with CDC, CDC users, or CDC outcomes. Rewrite each to lead with CDC."*

### Step 8 — Hand off to your section lead
Submit the draft with a short note covering: which win themes you threaded, which PWS sub-requirements you addressed, any open questions, and any places where you used a placeholder (e.g., for Key Personnel, past performance specifics) so the lead knows what needs filling.

### Things AI tools will get wrong unless you correct them
- They will use buzzwords no matter what. Always run Step 6.
- They will lead with "Deloitte" or "Our approach is to..." Always run Step 7.
- They will invent numbers and stats. Always cross-check against Section 13.
- They will invent personnel names. Use placeholders like `[Service Desk Lead — TBD]` until capture confirms.
- They will write generic federal IT language unless you push them toward CDC specifics. Always anchor to PWS task numbers and CDC's real ticket data.
- They will treat HCD as a section. Push them to thread it through every task description.
- They will overclaim past performance. Every PP claim must be confirmed by capture lead before it stays in the draft.
